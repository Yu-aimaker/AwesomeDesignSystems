# Focused brand discovery

This original workflow draws inspiration from the focused interrogation approach described in [Matt Pocock's skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills). It does not copy or depend on that implementation.

Choose the next question by impact and uncertainty, not by completing a questionnaire.

| Decision | Useful question | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Who is using this, and what do they already understand? | Terminology, density, guidance |
| Job | What must they successfully do during their first visit? | Information hierarchy and primary action |
| Context | Are they browsing, deciding under pressure, or operating repeatedly? | Navigation, error prevention, feedback |
| Promise | What should be more credible here than with alternatives? | Evidence, copy, visual emphasis |
| Personality | Pick a real example of “warm” or “precise.” What part fits? | Type, shape, imagery, tone |
| Boundaries | What would make this feel unlike your brand? | Explicit non-goals |
| Trade-off | Would you choose faster scanning or more guided explanation here? | Layout and progressive disclosure |
| Constraints | Which assets, languages, devices or accessibility needs are fixed? | System constraints |

When answers conflict, reflect the conflict with a concrete scenario and ask which goal wins there. Stop interviewing when the primary task, audience, differentiator and direction are actionable. Record lower-impact unknowns as assumptions; do not turn discovery into endless permission seeking.

Decision record: **Question → evidence → decision → visual/behavioral rule → how to validate → confidence**.
